## 1. What was decided and why

During a two-round brainstorm for a new frontier project, Carlos accepted **"local generative music with consent and authorship"** as its own project (2026-11-02), overriding my recommendation to keep it as a miuzika feature. Reasons he gave weight to:

- The market has made "licensed training data" the norm in twelve months (Suno v6, Udio with UMG/Warner, Stable Audio 3, ElevenLabs Music). That gap is closed and is **not** the project.

- What remains open: **artist-level** consent (not label-level), **verifiable provenance** of generated audio, **lineage** of what a model and a LoRA produced, and a legally mandated **machine-readable marking** of AI audio in the EU. Nobody ships this for musicians working locally.

- C2PA is deployed in images and video (Adobe, Leica, Nikon, Google, Microsoft) and almost absent in audio. There is no open watermark evaluated on music. Both are real technical gaps.

- EU AI Act **Article 50** is in force since 2 August 2026 (grace period for already-deployed systems ends **2 December 2026**). Anyone generating audio with an open model in the EU must mark it. The authorship layer is therefore a legal obligation, not only an ethical stance, and that is the adoption argument.

Three ideas from the brainstorm were folded into this project: C2PA provenance for locally generated audio (idea 19), real-time browser instruments with WebGPU (idea 18, now phase 2), and Article 50 compliance tooling (idea 25).

Full reports (Spanish): `~/estate-of-the-art/reports/2026-11-02-musica-consentimiento.md` and `~/estate-of-the-art/reports/2026-11-02-ai-act-articulo-50.md`.

### 3.2 C2PA for audio

- **c2pa-rs** (Rust, Apache-2.0, maintained by the Content Authenticity Initiative) has asset handlers for **WAV** (RIFF), **MP3** and **FLAC** in releases 0.81–0.85 (2026). MP3 and FLAC embed the JUMBF manifest inside an **ID3v2 GEOB frame**; WAV goes through the RIFF handler. Bindings: c2pa-node, c2pa-python, `c2patool` CLI. There is also a Dart package (`c2pa_audio`) for WAV/MP3 interoperable with c2pa-rs.

- Spec: 2.2 is the stable one per a third-party tracker (May 2025); 2.3 (Jan 2026) and 2.4 exist, and the brainstorm report says 2.4 covers embedded manifests in audio incl. OGG. **Not confirmed from c2pa.org today**; check `spec.c2pa.org` before relying on OGG.

- No DAW plugin and no music-industry tooling exists. Platforms (streaming, social) still strip metadata on upload, so the manifest alone is not enough: hence the watermark (3.4) and the external timestamp (3.3).

### 3.3 Consent and timestamps

- **Training and data mining assertion:** the current label is **`cawg.training-mining`** (CAWG spec v1.1), not the old `c2pa.training-mining`. Entries: `cawg.data_mining`, `cawg.ai_inference`, `cawg.ai_generative_training`, each `allowed | notAllowed | constrained`. CAWG Identity Assertion 1.2 was DIF-ratified 15-Dec-2025. It is a **signal, not an enforceable right**; say so in the docs.

- **TDMRep** (W3C Community Group report, CG-FINAL 2024-05-10): `tdm-reservation` / `tdm-policy` via HTTP header, `/.well-known/tdmrep.json` or HTML meta. In Apr-2025 the group discussed moving to W3C Recommendation and ISO; not confirmed as done. It is the mechanism Art. 4 DSM / art. 67 bis TRLPI rights reservation maps onto, so publish it for anything we host.

- **Spawning Do Not Train registry:** site was "under maintenance" at last check (Sept-2025 activity); no public API documentation found. Treat as optional, not as a dependency.

- **OpenTimestamps:** protocol works; reference server repo last pushed Jan-2024; public calendars `*.calendar.opentimestamps.org`, `*.calendar.eternitywall.com`, `*.calendar.catallaxy.com`; third-party clients still being released in 2026 (PHP, Dart, Ruby). Low-maintenance risk accepted: an `.ots` proof is verifiable against Bitcoin forever without the calendars. Alternative or complement: a Sigstore/Rekor-style transparency log. **No NFT, no token, no blockchain beyond the OTS anchor** (Carlos's decision).

### 3.4 Watermarking: the research gap

- **AudioSeal** (Meta, MIT): designed and evaluated for **speech** at 16/24 kHz; last release Dec-2024. Cheap (≈1.1 GFLOP/s).

- **SilentCipher**, **WavMark**, **Timbre**: open; WavMark is the least robust (Gaussian noise or MP3 can remove it). SilentCipher/WavMark cost ≈10× AudioSeal.

- **SoK "How robust is audio watermarking in generative AI models?"** (arXiv 2503.19176): reproduced 9 schemes, found 8 new attacks effective against all of them. Use its attack pipeline as the baseline evaluation.

- **MusicMark** (arXiv 2607.11117, Jul-2026 preprint): generative watermark embedded in the semantic latent of the music generator; reports AudioSeal-M and SilentCipher collapsing to 0.005 and 0.127 accuracy under cut + cover-song attack. Authors' own numbers; code availability unknown. Closest competitor to "open music watermark"; check whether weights are released before investing.

- **audiowmark** (GPLv3, non-neural, Debian 0.6.5 Jan-2026): classical spread-spectrum with FEC, survives MP3/Ogg. Pragmatic baseline for day one; not robust to remix/cover.

- **No 2026 benchmark ranks these on music.** Building that benchmark (music datasets, pitch/tempo/cover/remix attacks, perceptual quality) is itself a publishable contribution and is what the Lambda credits are for.

### 3.5 Article 50 obligations (from the report; not re-verified today)

In force 2-Aug-2026; deployed systems must comply by 2-Dec-2026. Commission Code of Practice final 10-Jun-2026 (~190 signatories): secure metadata (C2PA) **plus** watermark, plus visible labelling icons. Open compliance kits exist (EU AI Act Compliance Kit, Apache-2.0; EuConform) for the paperwork; nothing marks the output of self-hosted open models. AESIA is the Spanish authority, "guidance first".

## 4. Decisions already taken (do not reopen)

1. It is a **project of its own**, closely coupled to miuzika, not only a miuzika feature.

2. **No NFT / no blockchain** beyond OpenTimestamps anchoring. Rationale recorded: NFTs do not prove authorship, do not express consent or lineage, and musicians reject them.

3. **Local-first**: generation, signing and marking run on the user's machine (or on the user's own Lambda instance through miuzika's existing GPU path). No cloud service of ours in the loop.

4. The authorship layer has **four pieces**: (a) signature with the artist's key + public timestamp; (b) embedded C2PA content credentials (who, which tool, which stems, which AI help); (c) machine-readable training consent (`cawg.training-mining`, TDMRep, optionally Spawning); (d) lineage manifest of generated output (model, LoRA, input stems). It does **not** attempt statistical attribution ("3 % of this came from you"): that is unsolved research and we say so.

5. **Phase 1** = local generation + LoRA on the user's own stems ("train on your stems": consent is trivial because it is their music). **Phase 2** = real-time instruments/agents in the browser (RAVE-, Magenta RealTime-class models under WebGPU + AudioWorklet) inside miuzika.

6. **Open music watermark** is the designated research track and the likely most original contribution; uses the Lambda credits.

7. Docs and code in **English**; names in English.

## 6. Suggested first steps (ordered; adjust with Carlos)

1. **Read the laptop state** (`ESTADO.md` section 2 lists browser checks Carlos still has to do; don't pile new work on top of unheard regressions without asking).

2. **Decide shape and name** (section 7). My recommendation: a standalone repo exposing a Rust or TypeScript library plus a CLI (`sign`, `stamp`, `mark`, `verify`, `generate`), consumed by miuzika through its server. Rust fits c2pa-rs natively and Carlos's profile; TypeScript fits miuzika's stack. Pick one, write `CONCEPT.md` in English.

3. **Spike B (authorship), 1–2 days, no GPU:** take a WAV exported from miuzika; sign a C2PA manifest with `c2patool`/c2pa-rs including `cawg.training-mining` (notAllowed for generative training, allowed for inference, say) and a custom lineage assertion; stamp the file hash with OpenTimestamps; verify both; confirm the manifest survives WAV→MP3→FLAC re-encoding via the ID3v2 path and record what strips it. This produces the first honest demo.

4. **Spike A (generation), on Lambda:** run ACE-Step 1.5 (2B) and Stable Audio 3 Small; train one LoRA from a handful of Carlos's own stems; measure VRAM, time and cost; emit the lineage manifest from step 3 automatically. Decide which model is default.

5. **Watermark baseline:** wire **audiowmark** into the export path now (works today, GPL compatibility to check against the chosen license), then stand up the **evaluation harness** from the SoK attack pipeline on a music set (FMA/CC0 material; miuzika's VCSL library is CC0). Compare AudioSeal, SilentCipher, audiowmark, and MusicMark if its code exists. Only after that decide whether to train a music-specific watermark.

6. **miuzika integration:** `exportar` signs/stamps/marks by default with a visible toggle; agent tool `generar_audio(prompt, lora?, duration)`; lineage shown in the project; Spanish/English strings via `i18n.ts`.
